Withdrawing takes cash out of oka and puts it somewhere only you control. It is the one action where you sign, every time.

## How it works

Pick a destination chain, type an amount, paste an address, confirm.

Your cash lives as USDC on Base. Withdraw to Base and it is a plain transfer. Withdraw anywhere else and it is bridged out, which the quote prices before you sign.

<Warning>
  **An onchain send cannot be reversed.** Check the address. oka warns you if you paste one of your own oka wallets, but it cannot know whether an address you own on the other side is the one you meant.
</Warning>

## You sign it

Deposits, trades and bridges run without asking you: a session key signs them, which is what makes the app feel like an app. Withdrawals do not use it. Moving money out of oka asks for your wallet, every time, and the request expires if it goes unanswered.

That is the line. Everything that keeps money inside the system is automatic; the one action that takes money out is yours alone to authorise.

## Minimums and cost

**$2** on Solana, Base, Arbitrum, BSC and Robinhood Chain. **$25** on Ethereum, where gas makes anything smaller pointless.

oka charges nothing to withdraw. You pay the network, and the bridge when the destination is not Base. The figures shown are indicative: a cent on Solana, a couple on Base, a few dollars on Ethereum.

## Getting wrappers out

Withdrawals move cash, not positions. To take value out of a stock you hold, sell it first: the proceeds land as cash on Base, and that is what you withdraw.

Your keys are the other exit. Both wallets are exportable from the account menu, and an exported key reaches everything in them directly, oka included or not.
