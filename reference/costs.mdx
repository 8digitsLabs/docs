---
title: "What it costs"
description: "Every line between your stables and your position, who receives it, and which ones oka can do anything about."
---

Three things can appear between your stables and your position, and one of them only once. The order ticket names each before you confirm, so the all-in figure is never a number you have to take on faith.

## Getting money in

**$1 per deposit**, flat: it repatriates your funds to Base, where oka operates, which is what keeps the fees on your trades low. Deposits already on Base are free.

<Note>
  Above **$10,000** a deposit can carry up to an extra **0.1%**, disclosed before you send.
</Note>

Minimums: **$10** on Solana, Base, Arbitrum and BSC, **$20** on Ethereum and Robinhood Chain.

## The spread

The difference between the pool's mid price and what you actually get, price impact included. It goes to the market, not to oka, and it is the largest cost on most trades.

It is also the one routing exists to attack: comparing listings is mostly comparing spreads and depth. A pool that is thin for your size costs more than one whose headline price looks worse.

## The gas take

Chains charge gas in their own token, ETH, SOL, BNB, and you hold stables. oka pays the gas and charges you the equivalent in stables, so you never keep a native balance on five chains just to be able to trade.

It is an estimate made before the fact, with a **20% buffer**, because gas moves between the quote and the transaction landing. There is a floor of **$0.01** per trade so the amount is never dust; on Solana, where fees are tiny and predictable, it is a flat **$0.005**. Some venues settle through a solver who pays the gas themselves, on those fills there is no gas take at all.

It scales with the chain, not with your order. That is what makes small trades on Ethereum unattractive and why its minimum is higher.

## The bridge fee

Charged when a route leaves Base to reach a listing, and again on the way home from a sale. It goes to the bridge, and it is inside the number the ranking compares: a listing that is expensive to reach loses on the total, not on its sticker.

## What oka takes today

**Nothing.** No platform fee is charged on a trade. The two lines above are passed through: gas to the chain, bridge to the bridge.

<Note>
  **Planned: 0.25% platform fee.** A quarter of a percent on a trade, of which **40% goes to whoever referred you**, and to the platform when nobody did. It is not charged today and does not appear on any ticket.
</Note>

## Execution tolerance

A fill is guaranteed against the quoted output **minus 100 basis points**, not against the quoted price to the cent. Between confirming and landing, the pool moves; that band is what stops a trade reverting on a one-tick drift. Past it, the trade fails and nothing is spent.

## What is free

Holding a position. Having an account. Exporting your keys. Withdrawing costs you the network's gas and nothing to oka.

<Note>
  Minimum trade sizes exist because of gas, not margin: **$1** on Solana, Base, Arbitrum and Robinhood Chain, **$2** on BSC, **$10** on Ethereum. They apply per listing rather than per order: a trade too small for Ethereum simply stops considering Ethereum listings.
</Note>
