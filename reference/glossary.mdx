---
title: "Glossary"
description: "The words oka uses, and what each one means precisely."
---

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Underlying">
    The real security, AAPL, the share Apple issues. It trades on a stock exchange during that exchange's hours. You never hold it on oka.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Wrapper">
    A token an issuer mints to track an underlying. AAPLx by xStocks tracks AAPL. Your claim is on the issuer, not on Apple. Two wrappers on the same stock are different instruments.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Listing">
    One wrapper deployed on one chain, AAPLx on Solana. Each listing has its own pool, depth and price. This is the unit oka trades, and what Auto mode ranks.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Exposure">
    What you hold after a trade settles: a wrapper balance whose value follows the underlying's price. Not legal title, no vote, no entry on any shareholder register.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Cash">
    USDC on Base, unspent. Deposits are swept here whichever chain they arrived from, sale proceeds come home here, and a failed order leaves your money here. Always spent before oka moves anything new in.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Execution wallet">
    The two wallets created at sign-in, one EVM, one Solana, presented as one. Where cash sits and wrappers land. Either can be exported from the account menu.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Session key">
    What lets oka execute the steps of a trade, sweeping a deposit to Base, filling, exiting, bringing a failed route home, without prompting at each one. Attached automatically at sign-in, with no prompt and no revoke button. It does not cover withdrawals.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Perp">
    A contract that tracks a reference price with no expiry date, matched on Lighter. You post margin, pick a side and a multiple, and your profit follows the reference. You own nothing: no wrapper, no issuer, no claim on anything. Long profits when the reference rises, short when it falls.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Perps balance">
    Stables credited to your perps account on Lighter, usable as margin and for nothing else. A third balance beside your cash and your positions, and it genuinely sits elsewhere: cash is in your own execution wallet, this is on Lighter. It cannot buy a stock, and moving between the two is a real transfer of a few minutes rather than a book entry. Split into collateral, which is all of it, and free margin, which is the part not backing a position and the only part that can leave.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Margin">
    The stables backing an open perp. What you stand to lose, and the thing leverage multiplies against.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Leverage">
    The multiple of your margin a position controls. At 10x, $100 of margin controls $1,000. It multiplies the gain, the loss and the fee by the same factor, and divides the distance to liquidation by it. The ceiling is set per market and runs from 1x to 50x.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Liquidation price">
    The reference price at which your margin no longer covers the loss, so the position is closed and the margin is gone. Not a fee and not a penalty, and nothing recovers from it. Shown before you confirm and while the position is open.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Funding">
    A payment exchanged every 8 hours between longs and shorts, which is what holds a perp near its reference when it has no expiry to force the two together. Above the reference, longs pay. Below it, shorts pay. It is the cost of holding, and owning a wrapper has no equivalent.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Cross and isolated">
    How margin is shared. Cross backs every position with your whole balance, so one can be saved by the others and sunk by them. Isolated risks only what you allocated to that position.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="All-in price">
    Everything you pay between the cash you hold and the position you end up with: spread and price impact, the bridge when the route needs one, and the gas take.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Fill">
    A buy. Cash in, wrapper out. All-or-nothing: it settles close to the quote or it does not happen.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Exit">
    A sale. Wrapper out, proceeds bridged home to Base as cash. Large exits can be split across several listings, and those legs settle independently.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Platform fee">
    0.25% on a trade, of which 40% goes to whoever referred you. The only line oka keeps.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Gas take">
    What oka charges in stables to cover the native-token gas it paid on your behalf, so you never hold ETH, SOL or BNB. Scales with the chain, not with your order size.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Auto mode">
    The default. oka compares every listing that can fill and takes the one leaving you the most stock. You do not pick the issuer or the chain.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Expert mode">
    You choose the issuer and the chain from a table of every listing and its all-in cost. The execution venue stays out of your hands in both modes.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="OKA">
    Has a key role in the governance of the index fund. You buy and sell it for IOKA, so its price follows the five stocks in the fund. Minted once. Supply only falls.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="IOKA">
    The index fund: five tokenized stocks in one token. OKA is bought and sold for IOKA.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Clearing price">
    The price of the last token sold in the sealed-bid auction. Every filled bid pays it, not the higher price that was bid. Bids above it are filled in full, bids exactly at it share what is left, bids below it are refunded.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Settlement token">
    The stablecoin a chain settles in. USDC everywhere, except Robinhood Chain, which uses USDG: the Global Dollar.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
