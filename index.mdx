---
title: "What oka is"
description: "Buy exposure to a listed stock with stablecoins, at the cheapest all-in price across issuers and chains."
---

oka lets you buy tokenized stocks with stablecoins. You sign in with an email address or a social account, deposit stables from any wallet, and place an order. oka prices the same stock across every issuer and every chain that lists it, and fills the cheapest one — all-in, fees included.

The screen you land on shows one number: what you hold, in dollars. Not a list of chains, not a wallet per network. Those exist underneath, and you can open them, but they are not the point.

## What you are actually buying

Not a share. When you buy AAPL on oka, you receive a **wrapper**: a token an issuer mints to track Apple's price. Several issuers do this, on different chains, at slightly different prices — which is the problem oka exists to solve.

You get economic exposure to the price. You do not get legal title, a vote, or an entry on any shareholder register.

## Why the cheapest listing is not obvious

The same stock can be a dozen different tokens. xStocks mints one on Solana, Ondo mints another, Coinbase and Robinhood mint their own on their own chains. They trade in separate pools, so at any moment they trade at different prices, with different depth and different spreads.

Buying the wrong one costs you real money before the stock has moved at all. oka simulates the whole path for every open listing — moving your stables to the right chain, the swap itself, the gas, the fee — and takes the one that ends up cheapest.
