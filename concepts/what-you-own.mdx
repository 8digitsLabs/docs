The same stock can be a dozen different tokens. These three words are what let you tell them apart.

## Underlying

The real security: **AAPL**, the share Apple issues. It trades on a stock exchange, during that exchange's hours, and it is what everyone means when they say "Apple stock". You never touch it on oka.

## Wrapper

A token an issuer mints to track an underlying. **AAPLx** by xStocks tracks AAPL. It is a claim on the issuer, not on Apple.

Two wrappers on the same underlying are not interchangeable. They have different issuers, different terms, different liquidity, and they trade at different prices.

## Listing

One wrapper deployed on one chain. **AAPLx on Solana** is a listing; the same wrapper on another chain is a different listing, with its own pool, its own depth and its own price.

This is the unit oka trades, and what Auto mode ranks when it picks "the cheapest".

<Note>
  Underlying → wrapper → listing is a widening: one underlying has several wrappers, and one wrapper has several listings. The catalog covers over 700 underlyings across 962 wrappers and close to 3,000 listings.
</Note>

## Exposure

What you hold once the trade settles: a wrapper balance whose dollar value follows the underlying's price.

It is not a share. No legal title, no vote, no entry on Apple's register. Your claim is on the issuer, and if that issuer stops honouring its token, the price of the underlying stops being the thing that matters.

## Cash

Stables sitting in your execution wallet, unspent. Deposits land here, sale proceeds come back here, and a failed order leaves your money here.

Cash lives as USDC on Base, whichever chain it arrived from, and is always spent before oka moves anything new in, which is why a retry after a failed fill costs less than the first attempt.
