---
title: "Routing"
description: "Why the cheapest headline price is usually not the cheapest trade, and what oka compares before quoting you one."
---

A listing's price tells you little about what buying it will cost. oka computes the part the price leaves out.

## What gets compared

Every order starts from the same place: **USDC on Base**. For each listing that carries the stock you asked for, oka prices the whole path from there.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Reach the chain">
    The listing may live on Solana while your cash is on Base. The bridge costs a fee and takes time, and both count against that listing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Cross the pool">
    The swap itself: mid price, spread, and the price impact your size causes. A thin book turns a good quote into a bad fill, and size is what exposes it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pay the gas">
    Every transaction burns gas on a chain whose fees have nothing to do with the stock. Ethereum's gas can exceed a small trade's entire spread.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The first two are inside what each listing returns, so the ranking compares them directly: listings are ordered by **how much stock you end up with**, and a bridge you had to pay for shows up as less stock. The gas take is quoted alongside, as its own line on the ticket.

Add them up and you get the all-in figure the ticket shows. A cent of spread advantage does not survive a three-dollar bridge.

## Who actually quotes

oka does not run its own pools for tokenized stocks. It asks the venues that do: **CoW Protocol** and **Mobula** on EVM chains, **Jupiter** on Solana, **LiFi** where it reaches further. Bridging goes through **Relay**.

Several venues may quote the same listing. oka keeps the best answer per listing and compares listings, leaving the choice of pool to the aggregator.

<Note>
  Some venues settle through a solver who pays the gas themselves. On those fills there is no gas take, which can flip the ranking between two listings that looked identical.
</Note>

## What drops out

**Quotes that are too good.** A fill fourteen times better than the market is a bug, so implausible answers are dropped.

**Listings too small to be worth it.** The minimum trade size applies per listing rather than per order. A \$7 order is not refused for being under Ethereum's \$10 floor: it stops considering Ethereum listings and fills from what is left.

**Listings whose venue says the market is closed.** Wrappers track stock-market hours, and a venue that will not quote outside them takes its listing out of the running.

**Routes that keep failing.** A listing that answers "no route" three times in a row leaves the comparison for an hour, and a venue that fails twenty times leaves entirely. A listing can therefore disappear for reasons unrelated to hours or liquidity.

## Auto and Expert

**Auto** picks the cheapest and does not present the alternatives it rejected.

**Expert** shows every listing side by side with its all-in cost and lets you choose the issuer and the chain. Choose it when you have a reason to hold one issuer's paper over another's.

An Expert pick is honoured only if it can execute. On a sell that needs several listings, the picker is hidden.

## When an order fails

A buy fills or it does not happen. There is no partial fill.

When it fails, the money stops as cash on Base. If the stables had already bridged to reach a listing that then failed, they are brought home. A retry re-runs the whole comparison and may pick a different listing, because the ranking has moved.

<Warning>
  One case does not resolve cleanly: a fill that lands while the trip home fails. The wrapper is yours, on that chain, and oka names it in your activity.
</Warning>

## Splitting a sale

Buys take one listing. Large sells sometimes cannot, so oka splits the exit across several: the fewest legs that cover the amount, emptying your smallest lots first.

Those legs execute independently, which is the one case where a result lands in between: `completed_partial`, with the activity screen naming which legs settled.
