Signing in with an email address creates a wallet. That sentence hides a fair amount, so here is the whole of it.

## The execution wallet

At sign-in oka creates two wallets and treats them as one: an **EVM wallet**, which covers Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, BSC and Robinhood Chain, and a **Solana wallet**. Together they are your execution wallet — where your cash sits, where your wrappers land, and what every order spends from.

You never pick between them. The app shows one balance and reaches for whichever half a given trade needs.

Sign-in is email, Google, X, or a passkey. There is no extension to install and no seed phrase to copy down before you can do anything.

<Note>
  A passkey is added to an account that already exists, from the account menu. It is a way to sign in faster next time, not a way to create the account in the first place.
</Note>

## Whose keys these are

Yours. Both wallets can be **exported** from the account menu — EVM and Solana separately — and taking a key out gives you that wallet itself, wrappers included, in any client you like.

oka never reads them. Export runs inside the key provider's own interface; the app hands off and gets nothing back.

This is the answer to "what if oka disappears". Your positions are tokens in a wallet whose key you can hold. Nothing about recovering them requires us to still be here.

## The session key

Approving every step of a route would make the product unusable: a buy can involve a bridge, an approval and a swap, and prompting four times for one order is not security, it is attrition.

So oka holds a **session key** on your wallets, scoped to the actions that make up trading — sweeping a deposit to Base, filling an order, exiting a position, bringing a failed route home.

<Warning>
  It is attached automatically when you sign in, without a prompt, and there is no button to revoke it. Signing out does not remove it, and neither does exporting your keys. Moving funds to a wallet oka does not hold is the way to end it.
</Warning>

What it deliberately does not cover: **withdrawals**. The one action that sends money to an address outside your wallet asks you to sign, every single time, and the request expires unanswered. A key that could move money away from you would be a key worth stealing.

## Wallets you already have

You can connect an external wallet — the one you already use — to fund your account. It transfers stables into your execution wallet, same chain, signed by you.

That connection is for deposits. It does not become your login, and oka does not trade out of it.
