Buying a wrapper gives you the stock's price and nothing else: no leverage, no way to profit from a fall, and nothing to trade while the exchange is shut. A perp is the other route. It is a separate product on a separate balance, and it works nothing like a fill.

<Note>
  Perps are for underlyings where leverage, a short, or round-the-clock access is the point. If none of those is what you want, buy the wrapper.
</Note>

## What a perp is

A perpetual future: a contract whose price tracks a reference, with no expiry date. You post margin in stables, pick a side and a multiple, and your profit or loss follows the reference from there.

You own nothing. There is no wrapper, no issuer and no claim on anything. That cuts both ways: no issuer can fail on you, and there is no asset to hold if the position closes.

**Long** profits when the reference rises. **Short** profits when it falls. Both are one order.

Crypto perps are not on oka and will not be. The universe is curated by hand, one entry at a time, and a market has to be a real-world reference to enter it.

## Leverage, and what it costs you

Leverage is a multiple on your margin. At 10x, \$100 of margin controls a \$1,000 position, and a 1% move in the reference is a 10% move in your money.

The ceiling is set per market, by how deep that market is, and it runs from 1x to 50x. The number is on each market's row.

<Warning>
  Leverage cuts the distance to liquidation by the same factor it multiplies your gains. At 20x, a 5% move against you is your whole margin.
</Warning>

## Liquidation

Every open position has a **liquidation price**. If the reference reaches it, the position is closed and the margin backing it is gone.

It is not a fee or a penalty, and nothing recovers from it. It is the price at which your margin no longer covers your loss, so the position stops existing. The ticket shows it before you confirm, and the position shows it while it is open.

Two things move it: the size of your margin, and your leverage. Nothing else. Adding margin pushes it away, raising leverage pulls it closer.

## Funding

A perp has no expiry, so something has to keep it near its reference. That something is **funding**: a payment exchanged every 8 hours between the two sides.

When the perp trades above its reference, longs pay shorts. When it trades below, shorts pay longs. The rate is small and it moves.

It is the cost of holding, and it has no equivalent when you own a wrapper. A position held for weeks pays it dozens of times.

## Perps balance

Margin comes from a **perps balance**, and it is a third balance rather than a corner of your cash.

You have cash, you have positions in stocks, and you have this. Cash buys stocks. Perps balance backs perps. Neither can do the other's job: a perps balance cannot pay for a wrapper, and cash cannot open a position without moving first.

### Why it is separate

It is not a design choice oka made for tidiness. The money genuinely lives somewhere else.

Your cash sits in your own execution wallet, where you hold the keys and can export them. A perps balance sits on the venue that matches the orders, credited to an account anchored to your wallet address. Showing them as one number would tell you that a single figure is available for anything, when half of it is two transfers and a wait away from being usable.

### Moving money in

Transferring from cash sends stables from your wallet to your own funding address, and the venue credits the account a few minutes later. The minimum is \$3.

The first transfer is what creates the account. There is no sign-up step and nothing to approve: the money arriving is the registration.

<Note>
  oka charges nothing to move money either way. The route still bridges, so a little of it is lost in transit: the amount that will arrive is estimated before you confirm, not after.
</Note>

### Collateral and free margin

Once you have a position open, the balance splits in two.

**Collateral** is everything on the account. **Free margin** is the part not currently backing a position, and it is the only part that can open a new one or leave.

Opening a position moves margin from free to committed. Closing it moves the margin back, plus or minus what the position made. A liquidation moves it nowhere: that margin is gone.

### Moving money out

A withdrawal is capped at free margin, never at collateral. Margin backing an open position cannot leave without moving that position's liquidation price closer, so oka will not let it. Close the position first, or withdraw less.

Coming out is slower than going in, and it has more legs. It leaves the venue on the venue's own clock, arrives on Ethereum, and is then bridged home to Base as cash. oka presents it as one movement and handles the steps, but it is not instant and it is not a book entry.

<Warning>
  Plan the exit before you need it. Money in a perps balance is not money you can spend on a stock the same minute.
</Warning>

## Market and limit orders

**Market** fills now, against whatever is resting in the book. You get certainty of execution and pay for it.

**Limit** rests in the book at your price and waits. It may never fill, and you can cancel it at any time from the orders tab.

## Cross and isolated margin

**Cross** backs every position with your whole balance. One position can be saved by the others, and sunk by them.

**Isolated** risks only the margin you allocate to that position. It can be liquidated without touching the rest.

## Why a perp never appears next to a stock

You will not find a perp on a stock's page, in markets, in top movers, or in your watchlist. That is deliberate.

A wrapper is held and a perp can be liquidated. Putting them side by side for the same company makes leverage read as a setting on a purchase, which is the single most expensive misunderstanding available in this product. Perps live in their own section, and you get there on purpose.
