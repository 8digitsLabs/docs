**It governs the fund.** The fund is five tokenized stocks.

**It follows those stocks.** You buy and sell OKA for IOKA. When the five stocks rise, OKA is priced in a token that rose with them.

**Its supply only falls.** OKA is minted once. Nothing can mint more. What is burned stays burned.

## How OKA is distributed

| | Share | |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquidity** | 70% | The pools have to be deep enough to trade against. |
| **Community incentive** | 25% | Returned to people who use the app. Locked. |
| **Treasury** | 5% | Supports the protocol and the app as they grow. Locked. |

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The vault and the two pools">
    The five stocks are held in a vault. That contract is the fund. It keeps the stocks and issues IOKA against them, so one IOKA is a share of what the vault holds.

    ```mermaid
    flowchart LR
      USDG[Stables] <-->|"index market"| IOKA[IOKA]
      IOKA <-->|"OKA market"| OKA[OKA]
      VAULT[("Vault<br/>five tokenized stocks")] -.->|"backs"| IOKA
    ```

    Buying OKA with stables crosses both, left to right. The first hop is the market oka makes itself.

    **IOKA / USDG** is where the index trades against a stable. oka runs this pool. It quotes a band around the value of the basket and moves that band as the basket moves. When one side is bought out, the vault restocks it: buying the five stocks and minting IOKA, or selling them and burning IOKA.

    **OKA / IOKA** is where OKA trades, priced in the index. Liquidity stays in that one pool. The ratio of what the vault holds to IOKA in circulation is the **net asset value**.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Where the fees go">
    Buying OKA with stables crosses both pools, so it pays into both. The protocol's own share is the **growth fund**. It is what finances growing oka.

    **The OKA pool**

    ```mermaid
    flowchart LR
      P1(["OKA / IOKA pool"]) -->|"50% in OKA"| BURN["burned"]
      P1 -->|"50% in IOKA"| DIL["dilutes the index"]
    ```

    Half is taken in OKA and burned. That supply is gone. Nothing can mint it back.

    Half is taken in IOKA and dilutes the index. More IOKA stands against the same basket, so each IOKA is a smaller claim.

    **The index pool** charges **2%**. Half of that fee is taken in IOKA, half in USDG. Each half is split again, into the growth fund and a buyback of OKA.

    ```mermaid
    flowchart TB
      FEE(["IOKA / USDG pool<br/>2% fee"]) --> IOKA["50% in IOKA"]
      FEE --> USDG["50% in USDG"]
      IOKA -->|"0.5% of the trade"| GF1["growth fund"]
      IOKA -->|"0.5% of the trade"| BB1["broken down, then buys OKA"]
      USDG -->|"0.5% of the trade"| GF2["growth fund"]
      USDG -->|"0.5% of the trade"| BB2["buys OKA"]
    ```

    | Slice of the 2% | Of the trade | Where it goes |
    |---|---|---|
    | Half of the IOKA | 0.5% | Growth fund |
    | Half of the IOKA | 0.5% | Broken down, then used to buy OKA |
    | Half of the USDG | 0.5% | Growth fund |
    | Half of the USDG | 0.5% | Buys OKA |

    The IOKA headed for the buyback is broken down first, because a basket cannot buy OKA directly. The USDG can.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Market making">
    A Uniswap v4 hook and a bot make the market on the index pool, on top of the 2% fee.

    The hook quotes IOKA against USDG and holds that price to the fair value of the five stocks in the fund. The bot manages the reserves with the vault. When one side of the pool runs low, it buys the five stocks and mints IOKA, or sells them and burns IOKA, so the pool can keep quoting at that value.

    The fees generated by market making are used entirely to develop the project.
  </Accordion>
