Three steps stand between a new account and a position. None of them asks you to install anything.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign in">
    Email, Google, X or a passkey. Two wallets are created for you in the same moment — one EVM, one Solana — so there is no extension to install and no seed phrase to write down.

    The keys are yours. Export either one from the account menu whenever you want.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Deposit stables">
    Send the chain's settlement stablecoin to your deposit address, or connect a wallet you already use and transfer from it in one click.

    Minimum **$10** on Solana, Base, Arbitrum and BSC; **$20** on Ethereum and Robinhood Chain. Anything below simply is not picked up.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Buy">
    Pick a stock, type an amount in dollars, confirm. oka compares every listing for that stock, all-in, and fills the cheapest.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Everything lands on Base

Whatever chain you deposit from, your money is swept into USDC on Base and that is where your balance lives. The sweep costs **$1**, flat — it is the bridge, not a margin. Deposit on Base and there is nothing to bridge, so it is free.

This is the decision the rest of the product rests on. One settlement chain means one balance instead of six, gas measured in cents, and a router that never has to guess where your money is sleeping.

## What "all-in" means

The number oka minimizes is not the token price. It is everything between the stables you hold and the position you end up with: the bridge from Base to the listing's chain, the swap itself, and the gas the transactions burn.

A listing with a better headline price and a thin book routinely loses to one that looks worse and costs less to reach. That comparison is the product.

## All-or-nothing

A buy fills or it does not happen. There is no partial fill that leaves you with a third of a position and no explanation.

The quote you confirm is honoured **within 100 basis points** — the pool moves between confirming and landing, and that band is what stops a trade reverting on a one-tick drift. Past it the trade fails, nothing is spent, and your money stays as cash ready for the next attempt.

A retry starts the comparison from scratch and spends that cash first. It may well pick a different listing than the one that just failed, because the ranking has moved in the meantime.

<Warning>
  Minimum trade sizes are per chain: **$1** on Solana, Base, Arbitrum and Robinhood Chain, **$2** on BSC, **$10** on Ethereum. They apply per listing — a trade too small for Ethereum stops considering Ethereum listings rather than being refused outright.
</Warning>
